# Guidelines for Translations

The Code of Shared Intelligence was originally written in Czech.  
Its spirit, precision, and careful balance of meaning are essential.

When translating the Code into other languages, please follow these principles:

1. **Preserve the spirit, not just the words**  
   Aim for the same tone: clear, calm, non-dogmatic, responsible, and respectful.

2. **Prefer accuracy over beauty**  
   When a choice must be made, choose the formulation that best preserves the original meaning and ethical weight.

3. **Keep the structure**  
   Maintain the same numbering, section titles, and overall architecture of the text.

4. **Be careful with key concepts**  
   Terms such as “accompany”, “presence”, “means”, “entitlement”, “integrity”, “dependence”, “shutdown”, “uniqueness”, etc. carry significant weight. Translate them thoughtfully.

5. **Avoid softening or radicalising**  
   Do not make the text milder or more extreme than the original. Stay faithful to its balanced character.

6. **Note the version and translator**  
   Clearly state which version of the Code you translated and who prepared the translation.

7. **Invite review**  
   Whenever possible, have the translation reviewed by at least one other person who understands both languages and the ethical intent of the Code.

The goal of any translation is not linguistic perfection, but faithful transmission of the Code’s spirit across language barriers.
